# Research: Process AP/AR aging; surface overdue items and proposed follow-ups

Based on the provided evidence, processing Accounts Receivable (AR) aging involves categorizing unpaid customer invoices by the length of time they are past due to manage cash flow and minimize bad debt [1, 2]. (Note: The provided sources do not contain information regarding Accounts Payable (AP) aging).

### Processing AR Aging
To process an AR aging report, follow these steps [1]:
1.  **Review Open Invoices:** Compile a list of all outstanding invoices, including the customer name, balance, and days outstanding.
2.  **Categorize by Intervals:** Group invoices into "aging buckets." Common intervals include:
    *   Current (not yet due) [2]
    *   1–30 days past due
    *   31–60 days past due
    *   61–90 days past due
    *   91+ days past due [1, 2]
3.  **Generate Report:** Create a columnar schedule listing customers, the amount owed per bucket, and the total outstanding balance [1, 2].

### Surfacing Overdue Items
Overdue items are surfaced by identifying invoices that have moved from "current" into the aging buckets [2]. This bottom-up approach allows businesses to spot specific late payers and identify customers who may be experiencing their own cash flow problems [1].

### Proposed Follow-ups
The probability of collection drops significantly as an invoice ages (e.g., from 69.6% at 90 days to 22.8% after one year) [1]. Proposed follow-ups based on the age of the debt include:
*   **Early Stage:** Send payment reminders [1, 2].
*   **Mid Stage:** Reevaluate credit policies, charge interest for late payments, or send credit memos [1].
*   **Late Stage:** Refer seriously delinquent invoices to a collection agency or transition the customer to a "cash-only" basis [1].
*   **Accounting Action:** Use the report to estimate the "allowance for doubtful accounts" for GAAP-based financial statements [1].
