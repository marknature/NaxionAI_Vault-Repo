# Research: Triage business inbox; draft urgent replies; flag approval-required items

Based on the provided evidence, the process for triaging a business inbox, drafting urgent replies, and flagging items for approval is structured as follows:

### 1. Triage and Classification
The objective is to turn a mailbox into a "bounded queue of decisions" [1]. The process involves:
*   **Scope Setting:** Defining the account, folders, time window, and allowed actions (defaulting to read and draft, not send/delete) [1].
*   **Thread Retrieval:** Loading complete threads rather than single messages to ensure earlier unanswered questions are captured [1].
*   **Disposition Assignment:** Classifying threads into categories such as:
    *   **Urgent Reply:** Deadlines, blockers, customer risks, security, money, or executive requests [1].
    *   **Reply:** Direct questions or requests [1].
    *   **Action without Reply:** Scheduling, paying, or updating systems [1].
    *   **Waiting/Reference/Noise:** Tracking pending moves, useful info, or irrelevant mail [1].

### 2. Drafting Urgent Replies
To avoid a "generic-professional" tone, agents should:
*   **Calibrate Voice:** Study 20–50 of the user's recent sent replies to extract habits regarding greetings, sign-offs, formality, and sentence rhythm [1].
*   **Contextual Drafting:** Answer all material questions based on the thread and calibrated voice, avoiding invented commitments and stating uncertainty where applicable [1].

### 3. Flagging and Approval
To maintain security and accuracy, no external actions are taken without explicit approval:
*   **Approval Batch:** The agent presents a batch showing the recipient, action, draft summary, deadline, and risk [1].
*   **Verification:** Only after the user approves individually or as a batch does the agent send, label, or archive the mail [1].
*   **Confirmation:** The agent must read back the provider-confirmed results to verify the action was successful [1].
