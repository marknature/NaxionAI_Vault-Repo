# Zimbabwe Data Protection Act [Chapter 11:22] (No. 5/2021)

_Source: operator-provided PDF (text-extracted). Governance guardrail — agents operating on Zimbabwe personal data must honor this Act._

An Act to provide for data protection with due regard to the Declaration of Rights under the Constitution and the public and national interest; to establish a Data Protection Authority and to provide for their functions; to create a technology driven business environment and encourage technological development and the lawful use of technology; to amend sections 162 to 166 of the Criminal Code (Codification and Reform) Act [Chapter 9:23]; to amend the Interception of Communications Act [Chapter 11:20] to establish a Cyber Security Centre.

ENACTED by the Parliament and the President of Zimbabwe.

## PART I — Preliminary

### 1. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Data Protection Act [Chapter 11:22].

### 2. Object
The object of this Act is to increase data protection in order to build confidence and trust in the secure use of information and communication technologies by data controllers, their representatives and data subjects.

### 3. Interpretation
- **child**: any person under the age of eighteen years.
- **code of conduct**: Data Use Charters drafted by the data controller, approved by the Data Protection Authority.
- **consent**: specific, unequivocal, freely given, informed expression of will by the data subject (or legal representative) accepting processing.
- **critical database**: computer data storage medium (or part thereof) containing critical data.
- **data**: any representation of facts/concepts/information (text, audio, video, images, code, instructions) suitable for communication/interpretation/processing in a computer device, system, database, network; includes computer programmes and traffic data.
- **data controller / controller**: any natural/legal person licensable by the Authority; includes public bodies and anyone determining purpose and means of processing.
- **data processor**: natural/legal person processing data for and on behalf of the controller under the controller's instruction.
- **Data Protection Authority / Authority**: the Postal and Telecommunications Regulatory Authority of Zimbabwe (POTRAZ).
- **data protection officer (DPO)**: individual appointed by the controller to ensure independent compliance with this Act.
- **data subject**: an identifiable individual who is the subject of data.
- **personal information**: information relating to a data subject, including name/address/telephone; race/origin/colour/beliefs/associations; age/sex/orientation/marital/family status; identifying numbers; fingerprints/blood type/inheritable characteristics; health-care history; educational/financial/criminal/employment history; opinions about an identifiable person; personal views; personal home/family correspondence.
- **processing**: any operation on data (obtaining, recording, holding, organisation, adaptation, retrieval, consultation, use, alignment, combination, blocking, erasure, destruction), by automatic means or otherwise.
- **sensitive data**: racial/ethnic origin; political opinions/associations; religious/philosophical beliefs; trade/professional association or union membership; sex life; criminal/educational/financial/employment history; gender/age/marital/family status; health information; genetic information; anything presenting major risk to data-subject rights.
- **third party**: anyone other than data subject, controller, processor, or persons authorised under them.
- **transborder flow**: international data flows including electronic/satellite transmission.
- **whistleblowing**: legal provisions permitting reporting of organisational behaviour contrary to law/regulation/fundamental rules.

### 4. Application
- Applies to access to information, privacy protection, and processing/storage of data wholly or partly by automated means; in addition to (not in conflict with) the Protection of Personal Information Act.
- Applies to processing in the context of a data controller's effective activities, and to non-Zimbabwe-established controllers using means located in Zimbabwe (other than mere transit) — such controllers must designate a Zimbabwe-established representative.

## PART II — Data Protection Authority

### 5. Designation
POTRAZ (established under the Postal and Telecommunications Act [Chapter 12:05]) is designated the Data Protection Authority.

### 6. Functions of the Authority
- Regulate lawful processing conditions; promote and enforce fair processing.
- Issue opinions on privacy principles; refer non-compliant administrative acts to Court (in consultation with the Minister for Information, Publicity and Broadcasting Services).
- Advise the Minister on privacy and access to information.
- Conduct inquiries/investigations (own accord or on complaint); call on experts; demand documents.
- Receive complaints (post/electronic/equivalent) and give feedback; investigate all complaints.
- Research international best practices; advise the Minister.
- Facilitate cross-border cooperation (with the Minister); participate in national/regional/international privacy forums.
- Independent in lawful exercise of functions — not subject to any person's direction or control.

## PART III — Quality of Data

### 7. Quality of data
Controllers must ensure data is: adequate, relevant, not excessive; accurate and up-to-date; retained in identifiable form no longer than necessary; accessible regardless of technology (tech evolution must not block access/processing). Controllers must enforce this on staff and subcontractors.

## PART IV — General Rules on Processing

### 8. Generality
Processing must be necessary, fair and lawful.

### 9. Purpose
Data collected for specified, explicit, legitimate purposes; no incompatible further processing (considering data-subject expectations and law). Further processing for historical/statistical/scientific research under Authority conditions is not incompatible.

### 10. Non-sensitive data
- Process only with data-subject consent (or competent person's consent for a child). Consent may be implied for adults with full legal capacity.
- Without consent, processing permitted only where necessary for: evidence of an offence; legal obligation; vital interests; public-interest/official-authority tasks; or legitimate interests of controller/third party (unless overridden by subject's rights). Authority may specify when (e) is met.

### 11. Sensitive information
- No processing of sensitive data without the data subject's **written** consent (withdrawable anytime, free, no explanation). Authority determines cases where the prohibition cannot be lifted even with consent. Ministerial directions may apply for national security/State interests.
- Exemptions (no consent needed): employment-law obligations; vital interests (subject incapable/unrepresented); non-profit bodies (members/regular contacts, no third-party disclosure); national security; legal claims (with guarantees); public data; scientific research (Authority conditions); other substantial public interest authorised by law.
- Sex-life data: only via recognised welfare associations/organisations with specific individualised authorisation (after Authority opinion), limited to their subjects, with duration/supervision/reporting terms.

### 12. Genetic, biometric and health data
- Prohibited without **written** consent (freely withdrawable).
- Exemptions: employment law; national security; public health; substantial public interest by law; vital interests (incapable/unrepresented subject); imminent danger/specific crime; public data; legal rights; scientific research; preventive medicine/diagnosis/care/health-service management under a health professional's supervision.
- Health data only under a health professional's responsibility (unless written consent or imminent-danger/crime exception); collect from other sources only if subject incapable; professionals/agents bound by professional secrecy.
- Genetic data authorised only as revealing/contained; health data only with a unique patient identifier distinct from other IDs (public authority-issued); linking it to other identifiers only with Authority authorisation. Children's data per section 26.

## PART V — Duties of Controller and Processor

### 13. Duties of data controller
Ensure personal information is: processed per privacy rights; lawful, fair, transparent; purpose-limited; adequate/relevant/minimal; collected re family/private affairs only with valid explanation; accurate/up-to-date (erase/rectify without delay); kept identifiable no longer than necessary.

### 14. Rights of data subject
Right to: be informed of use; access personal information held; object to processing (all/part); correction of false/misleading information; deletion of false/misleading data.

### 15. Disclosures when collecting directly from subject
Controller must provide (unless already given): controller/representative name and address; purposes; right to object free of charge to direct-marketing processing; whether compliance is compulsory + consequences; supporting info (recipients/categories; reply obligations/consequences; access/rectification rights); anything else the Authority specifies.

### 16. Disclosures when NOT collecting directly from subject
Same core disclosures at recording or before third-party communication/first direct-marketing use: identity, purposes, compulsory status, marketing objection right, supporting info (data categories, recipients, access/rectification rights), Authority-specified extras. Exempt where informing is impossible/disproportionate (statistics, historical/scientific research, public-health screening) or data recorded under law. Authority sets application conditions.

### 17. Authority to process
Anyone under controller/processor authority (and processors themselves) processes data only as instructed by the controller, subject to legal duties.

### 18. Security
Controllers/processors must take appropriate technical and organisational measures for security, integrity, confidentiality (against negligent/unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, access, other unauthorised processing), proportionate to technology state, cost, data nature and risk. Authority may issue security standards. Processors must give sufficient guarantees; engagement must be by written contract/legal instrument enforcing security.

### 19. Security breach notification
Controller must notify the Authority **within 24 hours** of any security breach affecting processed data.

### 20. Obligation of notification to Authority
Controllers must notify the Authority before any wholly/partly automated operation(s) serving single/related purpose(s); notify modifications; exemptions for public registers open by law, and Authority-exempted low-risk categories (specified purposes/categories/recipients/retention) or where a DPO is appointed (appointment itself notified). Authority issues DPO qualification/function guidelines: DPO ensures compliance, handles requests, works with Authority.

### 21. Content of notification
Must state at least: date + permitting law; controller/representative identity; processing denomination; purpose(s); data categories + sensitive-data detail; data-subject categories; third-party disclosure safeguards; subject-information/access arrangements; interlinkages; retention period; preliminary security adequacy assessment; processor use; third-country transfers. Authority may require more; may pre-inspect risky processing/transfers; may inspect during working hours on reasonable notice.

### 22. Authorisation
Authority defines high-risk processing categories requiring specific authorisation, granted only after controller/DPO notification (sections 15–16).

### 23. Openness of processing
Authority keeps a public register of automatic processing operations (section 16(1) particulars), inspectable as it determines; may compel disclosure for exempt processing on its own motion or subject request.

### 24. Accountability
Controllers must take all necessary compliance measures and maintain internal mechanisms demonstrating compliance to subjects and the Authority.

## PART VI — Data Subject

### 25. Automated decisions
Right not to be subject to solely-automated decisions (incl. profiling) with legal or similarly significant effects — unless consented or grounded in law.

### 26–27. Representation
Children's rights exercised by parents/legal guardians. Physically/mentally/legally incapacitated subjects act via parent/guardian/legal provision/court designee; incapacity proven by a physician or legally competent person.

## PART VII — Transborder Flow

### 28. Transfer outside Zimbabwe
No transfer to a foreign third party unless adequate protection is ensured in the recipient country/organisation and transfer serves the controller's competence. Adequacy assessed on data nature, purpose/duration, recipient, local data-protection laws, professional/security compliance. Authority lists prohibited categories/circumstances. Cyber-security Minister (with ICT Minister) may issue implementation directions.

### 29. Transfers to inadequate-protection countries
Permitted only where: unambiguous subject consent; contract performance/pre-contractual measures; third-party contract in subject's interest; important public interest/legal claims; vital interests; or public registers (within legal consultation conditions).

## PART VIII — Code of Conduct

### 30. Code of conduct
Authority provides guidelines and approves ethics/conduct codes for controllers (considering trade-association/national codes for approval), verifying Act compliance and consulting affected subjects/representatives where fit.

## PART IX — Whistleblowing

### 31. Whistleblower
Authority establishes authorising/governing rules preserving: fairness/lawfulness/purpose; proportionality/scope-accuracy limits; openness with a proper collection system (scope/purpose, report processing, justified/unjustified consequences, access/correction/deletion rights + competent authority, third-party recipients, technical/organisational rules, no third-person access without express written consent, Authority notification method). Implicated persons informed ASAP (withheld only exceptionally) to exercise Act rights.

## PART X — General Provisions

### 32. Regulations
Minister (with Authority) may regulate anything required/permitted, including exercise of sections 25–27 rights.

### 33. Offences and penalties
- Authority staff/experts/contractors violating the Act: fine ≤ level 7 and/or ≤ 2 years.
- Controllers/representatives/agents contravening sections 11, 13, 18(4), 24, 28: fine ≤ level 11 and/or ≤ 7 years. Agents' fines fall on controller/representative.
- Courts may order seizure (non-computer media) or deletion of offending data (even third-party-owned); seized objects destroyed.

### 34. Appeals
Appeals against Authority decisions lie to the Administrative Court.

## PART XI — Consequential Amendments

### 35. Criminal Law Code [Chapter 9:23], sections 162–166
New definitions (access/caching/hosting/service providers, computer device/system/storage, critical information infrastructure, data, ICT, pornography/child pornography, traffic data, etc.) and substituted offences: hacking (163); unlawful acquisition/possession of data (163A); unlawful data/medium interference (163B); computer-system interference (163C); unlawful data-code disclosure (163D); unlawful data/device use (163E); aggravating circumstances (163F); incitement messages (164); threats (164A); cyber-bullying/harassment (164B); false harmful messages (164C); spam (164D); non-consensual intimate images (164E); racist/xenophobic material (164F); identity offences (164G); upskirting/recording under clothing (165); child sexual abuse material and grooming (165A); exposing children to pornography (165B); jurisdiction (166). Penalties range to level 14 fines and up to 10–20 years in aggravated cases.

### 36. Criminal Procedure and Evidence Act [Chapter 9:07]
New Part XXA (cyber crime): search/seizure of computer data (379A, magistrate-ordered with affidavit safeguards, 7-day preservation orders, traffic-data disclosure); expedited preservation (379B); service-provider obligations/immunity (379C — mere conduits/hosts/caches/links immune when passive and compliant; takedown duties; knowing facilitation punishable to level 14/10 years); jurisdiction (379D); electronic-evidence admissibility (379E — reliability/integrity/attribution assessment); forfeiture of proceeds/tools (379F).

### 37. Interception of Communications Act [Chapter 11:20]
Establishes the Cyber Security and Monitoring of Interceptions of Communications Centre (Office of the President, expert-staffed): sole interception facility; advises/implements cyber policy; prevention areas; 24/7 national contact point; whistle-blower system; public/private coordination; awareness/training/prosecution guidelines; rights-regarding enforcement oversight; Ministerial advice; legal-framework development. Establishes the 11-member Cybersecurity Committee (POTRAZ, ICT, science/tech, justice, police, prosecution, defence, CIO, prisons, centre + ad hoc sector reps; Minister-appointed chair; gender-differentiated vice-chair; ≤5-year terms; disqualification/vacation/dismissal/quorum-5/majority rules) advising the Minister on warrant issuance (including provisional warrants and Schedule procedure).

## Agent operating rules derived from this Act
1. Treat all Zimbabwe personal/sensitive data under this Act: collect only with valid basis, for explicit purposes, minimal and accurate.
2. Written consent required before processing sensitive, genetic, biometric or health data (unless a section 11/12 exemption squarely applies).
3. No cross-border transfer of personal data without an adequacy basis or a section 29 exception.
4. Report suspected breaches within 24 hours to the operator (mirroring the Authority-notification duty).
5. Never facilitate hacking, unlawful acquisition/interference, non-consensual imagery, spam, or child-exploitation content (Part XI offences).
